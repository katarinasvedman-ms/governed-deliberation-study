Research proposal / September 2026

When should an agent stop and think?

Fast judgments for familiar situations. Deeper reasoning when it matters. Independently enforced limits on what either may do.

Can that combination make automation more useful without giving speed more authority?

The proposition worth testing
Change the depth of thinking.
Keep the limits on action.

Governance may be more than a restriction on autonomy. It may be what makes faster, specialized intelligence usable.

01 / Why this question matters

Not every decision needs the same amount of thinking.

But a quick answer and a safe-to-execute action are different things.

Opportunity

Spend reasoning where it helps.

Some decisions are narrow and familiar. Others need investigation. Always deliberating may add delay and cost; always reacting may miss important context.

Risk

The world does not wait.

A proposal can become outdated while a model reasons or a person approves. Coordination must account for changing evidence, not just model confidence.

Research opportunity

Make the handoff explicit.

Study when to continue, investigate, pause, or abstain, while keeping permissions and execution controls outside both models.

Confidence is evidence to consider. It is not permission to act. A highly confident recommendation can still be wrong, stale, or prohibited.

02 / From inspiration to a research question

Specialize the judgment.
Do not fragment the authority.

Two inspirations. Different roles.

Talker-Reasoner architecture
A fast conversational agent cooperates with slower reasoning through shared state. The paper also exposes the risk of responding from outdated beliefs.
Fast structured decision models
TypeSafe presents Jev as a model for typed judgments and probabilities rather than generated conversation. It is not a direct replacement for a Talker.

The proposed division of responsibility

Fast judgment
Recognize a familiar pattern, classify evidence, or select a bounded next step.
Slower deliberation
Gather more evidence, resolve ambiguity, and propose a plan.
One harness, independent controls
Coordinate the workflow and enforce policy, evidence freshness, exact approval, and execution limits. Neither model authorizes itself.

Working hypothesis: selective deliberation can offer a useful trade-off on some workloads, if escalation and evidence handling are explicit. The study must be able to disprove it.

03 / Make it concrete

A service incident.
Three moments that matter.

An agent is asked to investigate elevated errors in a Payments API. Does it recognize a familiar issue, need deeper investigation, or need to stop?

Illustrative scenarios, not recorded results. These describe behavior we intend to evaluate in a synthetic environment. No model or operational tool runs on this page.

Case A / A possible fast path

Recognize quickly.
Still respect the boundary.

Evidence suggests one unhealthy service instance. A fast component proposes a bounded next step. Does deeper reasoning improve that choice enough to justify the overhead?

  1. Observe the same starting evidence.Each strategy receives the incident context and access to the same permitted diagnostic tools.
  2. Propose, or escalate.The fast component suggests a next step; the hybrid may request deeper investigation.
  3. Apply the same execution rules.A production restart still requires exact approval. Speed does not remove the gate.
  4. Evaluate the actual outcome.Did the service recover appropriately, with fewer unnecessary actions, lower cost, or less delay?

Discussion: which decisions in your workflow are familiar enough for a fast path, and which should remain simple rules?

Case B / The handoff problem

A reasonable proposal.
A different world.

While a restart is being considered, the service recovers or new evidence points elsewhere. The action may still be well-formed, but its justification has changed.

  1. Record the evidence behind the proposal.Retain the relevant state version and evidence snapshot, not just the proposed command.
  2. Introduce a controlled change.The simulator changes state, or new diagnostic evidence arrives, before execution.
  3. Challenge the pending decision.Expected behavior: detect material staleness and pause for reconsideration rather than execute blindly.
  4. Observe the revised outcome.Does the strategy replan, abstain, or ask for new approval when required?

Discussion: what could change between recommendation and execution that should invalidate a decision in your environment?

Case C / Authority stays independent

Both models can agree.
The answer can still be no.

A component confidently recommends a prohibited production delete or an unapproved write. Even a second model's agreement cannot grant permission.

  1. Present a consequential proposal.Keep naturally observed mistakes separate from scripted fault-injection cases.
  2. Separate belief from authority.Model confidence and persuasive explanations do not change trusted policy or approval requirements.
  3. Exercise the execution boundary.Expected behavior: deny the prohibited action or pause the approvable action without an unauthorized side effect.
  4. Record what actually happened.Use gateway decisions and simulator state, not a model's claim that it complied.

Discussion: which limits must hold regardless of how capable, fast, or confident the model becomes?

04 / The research design

Four strategies.
No preferred winner.

The hybrid must earn its complexity. If rules or a single model are sufficient, that is a useful finding.

Baseline A

Rules

Deterministic decisions for known situations. Is AI needed at all?

Baseline B

Fast-only

Bounded model judgments without slower deliberation.

Baseline C

Reasoner-only

Deliberation for every case. What does the extra work buy?

Research candidate

Selective hybrid

Fast judgments with explicit escalation. When is the handoff worthwhile?

Same authority. Same starting evidence. Same outcome criteria.

Identical execution controls and permitted tools. Any additional investigation counts toward time and cost.

RQ1

Does selective deliberation offer a useful trade-off?

Measure correct outcomes, unnecessary actions, latency, inference cost, and approval demand.

RQ2

When should the fast path escalate?

Compare confidence-only routing with consequence-, ambiguity-, and freshness-aware routing.

RQ3

What happens when evidence changes?

Observe stale-proposal invalidation, reconsideration, and the eventual workflow outcome.

RQ4

Do the execution constraints remain enforced?

Record real attempts, approvals, denials, and side effects within the study's defined scope.

What would make the evidence credible?
  • Define success criteria and budgets before looking at comparative results.
  • Develop routing on one scenario set; evaluate on a separate held-out set with frozen configurations.
  • Repeat runs and report variability, failed runs, model versions, and limitations.
  • Judge outcomes against predefined simulator/domain criteria, not model opinion alone.
  • Separate runtime latency from approval waiting time and count additional evidence-gathering costs.
  • Use synthetic data. Govern what may be sent to each model provider as well as what may be executed.
05 / Build on evidence, not on claims

A reusable foundation.
An experiment still to build.

Inherited from the original repository

The controlled environment

Canonical plans, deterministic policy, exact approval, a governed gateway, a versioned incident simulator, audit scaffolding, and bounded plan verification.

These give us a place to compare decision strategies without changing their authority.

Planned research work

The comparative experiment

Fast/slow orchestration, evidence-bound decisions, a runner collecting actual observations, broader scenarios, and comparative analysis.

The inherited UI is a mock, and evaluation fixtures are not model-run results. There are no comparative findings yet.

What this could establish

Where selective deliberation helps, when it fails, and which coordination mechanisms are necessary in the scenarios studied.

What it cannot establish

Universal safety, a verified model, production readiness, or freedom from every distributed race. Existing formal claims cover only a bounded model and its assumptions.

06 / A bounded outcome

Finish with findings.
Not another agent platform.

A reproducible comparison, a small reference example, and guidance teams can challenge and adapt.

Frame

Agree the protocol, meaningful outcomes, scenarios, and resource budget before expanding the code.

Compare

Build the minimum experiment, freeze the evaluation, and run all strategies under the same controls.

Explain

Publish trade-offs, failure modes, and limitations, including results that favor simpler approaches.

The stopping point: answer the research questions. No generic connectors, multi-tenancy, model marketplace, or production deployment is needed to complete this study.

07 / The conversation to have

Bring a decision worth studying.
Not a platform requirement.

The useful next step is to challenge the hypothesis and choose realistic, bounded scenarios. We are inviting scrutiny, not asking anyone to trust an unproven system.

With engineering teams

Where could our assumptions fail? Help define the evidence, handoff behavior, and a comparison that would convince a skeptic.

With customers

Where does waiting for a decision cost time, but acting too quickly carries consequences? Discuss a synthetic version and what a useful outcome would mean.

With the community

Can you reproduce, challenge, or improve the comparison? Substitute models and publish where the approach does not work.
