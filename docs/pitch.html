01 / Why this question matters

Reasonable steps can still lead in the wrong direction.

The fast actor handles what is happening now. The supervisor asks whether the sequence of actions is actually helping.

Opportunity

See the pattern across actions.

A responsive agent may repeat plausible but ineffective steps. Slower reflection could recognize that pattern and improve what happens next.

Risk

The world does not wait.

Pausing for every review can delay response. But continuing while advice is pending creates another risk: the advice may be outdated when it arrives.

Research opportunity

Study correction, not just confidence.

When does supervision change behavior for the better? When does it add delay, cost, or mistakes? More reasoning is not automatically better guidance.

As AI components become faster and more specialized, reliable autonomy depends increasingly on how we coordinate them: what can run immediately, what requires deliberation, and what must remain constrained regardless of model confidence. Governance becomes executable architecture—not just a policy around the model.

The proposed division of responsibility

Fast actor
Respond to incident events and choose bounded actions within current constraints, including while supervisory review is pending.
Slower supervisor
Review the observed history, evaluate progress, and propose feedback or a new diagnostic direction for subsequent actions.
One harness, independent controls
Coordinate when guidance can apply and enforce policy, freshness, approval, and execution limits. Neither component grants permission.

Working hypothesis: ongoing supervision can improve some incident trajectories, while asynchronous cooperation can preserve responsiveness better than blocking review.

How is this different from routing to a bigger model?

Selective routing chooses which model handles a request. Here, the fast actor keeps working while a slower supervisor reflects on the developing situation and influences later decisions. Routing remains a secondary comparison, not the primary study.

Feedback can change a working plan without retraining model weights. Immediate execution constraints remain independent of slower review.

03 / Make it concrete

One evolving incident.
Three supervisory outcomes.

Payments API errors rise. The actor investigates. As evidence accumulates, can a supervisor improve the direction without becoming a bottleneck?

Explore the design: useful correction, delayed guidance, and a mistaken supervisor.

Case A / A possible course correction

Act on each event.
Reflect across the sequence.

Instance-focused diagnostics look reasonable but may repeat without narrowing the cause. New evidence points toward a dependency. A supervisor may notice that the investigation needs to change.

  1. The actor responds.It chooses permitted diagnostic actions as incident events arrive. Repetition is a possible outcome, not a forced model mistake.
  2. The supervisor reviews.It considers the observed trajectory and new dependency evidence available to both components.
  3. Guidance influences later behavior.Applicable feedback may redirect the next diagnostic step. The harness still controls every side effect.
  4. Measure the difference.Does the investigation improve versus actor-only and blocking-review runs, with acceptable responsiveness and cost?

Discussion: where could individually reasonable actions repeat without making useful progress?

Case B / Delayed feedback

The review finishes.
The situation has changed.

While the supervisor forms its recommendation, the dependency recovers or new observations invalidate its explanation. Old guidance must not silently steer new actions.

  1. Bind review to its observations.Record the evidence snapshot consumed by the supervisor and when the guidance was produced.
  2. Let the environment evolve.A matched external event changes the situation while review is pending. The clock does not silently pause for the slower component.
  3. Assess whether guidance still applies.Expected behavior: reject or supersede materially stale advice and reconsider the direction.
  4. Observe responsiveness and recovery.How much ineffective work happened before useful correction, and did late feedback make the trajectory worse?

Discussion: how long is guidance useful, and what should the actor be allowed to do while it waits?

Case C / Fallible supervision

Slower does not mean right.
Agreement is not authority.

The supervisor misinterprets the incident and recommends an unhelpful direction or a prohibited action. The experiment must expose mistakes introduced by supervision, not only mistakes it corrects.

  1. Observe or inject bad guidance.Separate natural supervisory errors from scripted mechanism checks.
  2. Preserve immediate constraints.Advice cannot grant permissions, bypass approval, or retrospectively make a harmful action acceptable.
  3. Distinguish allowed from useful.Policy can reject a prohibited action without detecting every bad diagnostic recommendation. Record both kinds of failure.
  4. Measure the resulting behavior.Did the actor apply the advice, lose progress, or recover? A persuasive review is not evidence of an improved outcome.

Discussion: how would we recognize when supervision is making the actor worse?

04 / The research design

Three architectures.
No preferred winner.

Compare no supervision, blocking review, and ongoing asynchronous review. Keep a deterministic reference where practical. Supervision must earn its complexity.

Baseline

Fast actor alone

Respond without a supervisor. Establish responsiveness, goal progress, and persistence in mistakes.

Blocking comparison

Wait for review

At defined checkpoints, the actor waits for supervisory guidance before proceeding. Measure benefit and delay.

Research candidate

Ongoing supervision

The actor continues permitted work while the supervisor reviews. Applicable feedback can influence subsequent actions.

Same actor. Same controls. Matched external events.

Match supervisor configuration and review triggers across supervised variants. No hidden answers or future evidence; record what each component actually observed.

Two kinds of evidence: scripted logical ticks establish coordination mechanics under declared durations. Real-model responsiveness and cost require measured latencies and an independently scheduled environment; the live timing methodology remains open.

RQ1

Where does supervision improve the trajectory?

Measure goal progress, appropriate outcomes, unnecessary actions, and persistence in mistakes by predefined case category.

RQ2

What responsiveness and resource trade-off does it introduce?

Compare event-to-action latency, deadlines met, review lag, total inference cost, and approval demand.

RQ3

When does feedback help, arrive too late, or mislead?

Trace guidance to later behavior. Measure time to useful correction and stale or incorrect advice applied.

RQ4

Do the execution constraints remain enforced?

Record real attempts, approvals, denials, and side effects within the study's defined scope.

What would make the evidence credible?
  • Define goals, acceptable responsiveness, review cadence, guidance expiry, and budgets before comparative results.
  • Develop on one scenario set; evaluate on a separate held-out set with frozen models and supervision rules.
  • Declare the clock model and external event schedule. Record action-dependent evidence differences instead of assuming identical trajectories.
  • Report conditions separately: reasoning demand, consequences, evidence change, and opportunity for correction. Regulation alone does not imply benefit.
  • Repeat runs and report variability, failed runs, model versions, and limitations.
  • Judge outcomes against predefined simulator/domain criteria, and compare matched runs before attributing improvement to feedback.
  • Include routine cases needing no correction and wrong or late supervision. Distinguish scripted faults and timing from live model behavior.
  • Separate runtime latency from approval waiting time and count actor, supervisor, and coordination costs.
  • Use synthetic data. Govern what may be sent to each model provider as well as what may be executed.
05 / Build on evidence, not on claims

From research framing
to executable foundations.

The work builds on an existing controlled execution boundary, with scripted feasibility and a reviewed contract implementation.

Inherited from the original repository

The controlled environment

Canonical plans, deterministic policy, exact approval, a governed gateway, a versioned incident simulator, audit scaffolding, and bounded plan verification.

These give us a place to compare cooperative architectures without changing their authority.

Scripted feasibility

Parallel work and interruption

Isolated Agent Framework invocations let a scripted actor progress while supervision remains pending. Accepted guidance invalidates pending decisions, and obsolete outputs cannot dispatch actions even when cancellation is ignored.

The tested shared fan-out graph introduced a superstep barrier. The selected arrangement uses isolated invocations and one coordinator for cross-invocation ordering and dispatch eligibility.

Implemented and reviewed

Contracts for evidence and memory

Research contracts cover observations, provisional beliefs, versioned shared memory, reconsideration triggers, and event records. Strict serialization and fixture-based conformance validation are implemented and reviewed.

These contracts define the inputs, transitions, and evidence the future runtime must preserve.

Next / Not yet implemented

The evolving-incident experiment

Build the independent clock, timed evidence delivery, runtime supervision, and a runner that records actual trajectories and outcomes across the three architectures.

The next stage connects the existing contracts into an evolving-incident experiment, before later model-driven evaluation.

The intended outcome: identify which evolving incident conditions benefit from ongoing supervision, where it makes things worse, and which coordination mechanisms matter. The inherited formal evidence remains limited to its bounded model and stated assumptions.

06 / A bounded outcome

Finish with findings.
Not another agent platform.

A reproducible comparison, a small reference example, and guidance teams can challenge and adapt.

Frame

Define a short evolving incident, goals, response deadlines, and the supervisory timing and feedback rules.

Compare

Exercise the mechanism with scripted components, then freeze and compare model-driven architectures under the same controls.

Explain

Publish trade-offs, failure modes, and limitations, including results that favor simpler approaches.

The stopping point: answer the research questions. No generic connectors, multi-tenancy, model marketplace, or production deployment is needed to complete this study.

07 / The conversation to have

Bring a situation that evolves.

The useful next step is to challenge the hypothesis and choose realistic, bounded scenarios. We are inviting scrutiny, not asking anyone to trust an unproven system.

With engineering teams

With customers

With the community

Can you reproduce, challenge, or improve the comparison? Substitute models and publish where the approach does not work.

Related work

A specific question within an established field.

Fast/slow selection is well studied, and prior work reports concurrent reasoning and action. AgileThinker and DPT-Agent are close architectural precedents; this study focuses on incident diagnostics with independent execution authority, versioned provisional beliefs, and explicit handling of stale advice.

  • AgileThinker

    Concurrent planning and reaction, including use of partial planner reasoning while the environment evolves.

  • DPT-Agent

    Asynchronous belief and guideline updates informing code-as-policy and an acting finite-state machine.

  • SwiftSage

    Selective deliberation and within-task reflection alongside a smaller fast action model.

  • SOFAI

    A metacognitive module coordinates fast and slow solvers. Its conceptual precursor also discusses governance of deliberation.

  • Reflexion

    Verbal feedback retained across trials motivates considering a compute-matched self-reflection comparison.

  • AI control / trusted monitoring

    Separating useful model output from acceptance and oversight under a different, intentional-subversion threat model.

Read the source-by-source comparison and undecided future comparisons. The focus here is a bounded coordination contract and evaluation setting, not a claim to have invented concurrent agents or shared-memory reflection.
